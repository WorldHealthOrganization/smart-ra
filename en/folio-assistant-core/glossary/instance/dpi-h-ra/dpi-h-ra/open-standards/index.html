Open Standards candidate

Standards that are documented and made freely available to the public, which are developed, approved and maintained via a collaborative and consensus-driven process. Open standards facilitate interoperability and data exchange and are intended for widespread adoption (20).

Also called: open standards, Open standards

PHC handbook glossary wording, verbatim (who-iris/library/9789240093362-eng/sections/sec-002-glossary.md, p. 10), identical (with minor punctuation/citation differences) in PC handbook glossary (who-iris/library/9789240116191-eng/sections/page-008.md, p. 8) and SFP handbook glossary (who-iris/library/9789240120747-eng/sections/page-010.md, p. 10), and near-identical (two sentences) in SC handbook glossary (who-iris/library/9789240101197-eng/sections/page-009.md, p. 9): "Standards that are documented and made freely available to the public. Open standards are developed, approved and maintained via a collaborative and consensus-driven process. Open standards facilitate interoperability and data exchange and are intended for widespread adoption (10)." Substantive/complementary alternative in RA §2.2.2 (library/who-dpi-h-reference-architecture-draft-v1/sections/sec-022-222-the-enabling-conditions-for-reddhi.md, p. 35–39), citing Mehl et al. 2023 (library/mehl-2023-full-stac-oqad018/, linked via exactMatch to glossary/full-stac.glossary.json): "Open standards are validated and vetted specifications, repeatedly reusableacross contexts, that address common requirements, processes, and interactions and enable fidelity metrics." All are recorded as candidates; none is chosen (smart-ra#133 S1, smart-ra#139, smart-ra#142 item 3, smart-ra#143).
