Revenue raising (e.g., taxes, insurance contributions): Secure sufficient and sustainable funds for the health sector in an efficient and equitable way ()
An advanced macroeconomic and fiscal capability that establishes legal, administrative, and financial structures designed to pool financial resources directly for the health system. This capability utilizes a diversified mix of progressive tax mechanisms (such as dedicated payroll taxes, corporate levies, and "sin taxes" on tobacco or alcohol) alongside mandatory social health insurance contributions, ensuring that the collection architecture scales dynamically with the economy while protecting vulnerable populations from catastrophic out-of-pocket health expenditures. Its primary purpose is to decouple healthcare funding from unpredictable, short-term budget negotiations and establish a resilient, predictable revenue stream for long-term health planning. By aligning revenue collection with the principles of financial risk pooling, it ensures that wealthier individuals and profitable industries contribute proportionately more to the national health pool, allowing the state to provide comprehensive, high-quality care to all citizens regardless of their personal economic status. Example: A ministry of finance codifies a unified revenue raising capability for universal health coverage by introducing a 2% dedicated health levy on corporate profits alongside a mandatory, income-indexed health insurance contribution for all salaried workers; the system automatically routes these funds directly into a ring-fenced national health trust, providing a sustainable capital base that expands the public primary care network to rural communities without increasing the national deficit.
