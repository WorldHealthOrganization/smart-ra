Open data/ public dashboards ()
An advanced application service layer that automatically extracts, sanitizes, and formats internal health finance and performance data into intuitive, open-access public visualizations and downloadable datasets. This service exposes critical operational metrics to the public by mapping raw administrative databases into interactive charts, geographical spending maps, and searchable tables designed for non-technical users, civil society, and oversight journalists. Its primary purpose is to operationalize democratic transparency and build public trust in health system governance. By maintaining a continuous pipeline that strips out sensitive patient data while pushing aggregate expenditure and performance metrics into the public square, it crowdsources oversight, discourages the misappropriation of funds, and provides undeniable proof of how public resources are being converted into frontline healthcare delivery. Example: A municipal health department configures an open data and public dashboards service linked directly to its procurement database; every week, the system refreshes a public-facing map showing exactly how many diagnostic kits have been delivered to each neighborhood clinic, allowing local citizens to verify that their community clinic is receiving its fair share of medical resources.
