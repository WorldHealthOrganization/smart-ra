Public transparency dashboards published ()
An essential accountability outcome achieved when comprehensive, user-friendly, and machine-readable health expenditure data is automatically extracted from internal financial systems and published directly to open-access digital portals. This operational state ensures that citizens, civil society organizations, and journalists can freely inspect how public health funds are allocated, distributed, and spent across regions, facilities, and medical programs without needing specialized access credentials or freedom of information requests. Its primary purpose is to foster democratic oversight, combat corruption, and build public trust in the healthcare system. By translating dense, technical government ledgers into intuitive, highly visual charts and searchable tables, it exposes fiscal inefficiencies, empowers community-led monitoring, and holds public officials directly accountable for equitable resource distribution. Example: A ministry of health automatically updates its public transparency dashboard on the first of every month; local community advocates log onto the open portal to verify that the $500,000 newly allocated for their district's maternal health clinic was actually received and spent on local medical equipment rather than being diverted or delayed at the provincial level.
