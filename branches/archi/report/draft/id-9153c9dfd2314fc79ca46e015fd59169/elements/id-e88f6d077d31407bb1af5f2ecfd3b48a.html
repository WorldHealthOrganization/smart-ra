Traceability ()
The ability to track a product’s journey from origin to end-user – is crucial for non-profit and Public Health work. Whether distributing medicines, food aid, or other essential goods, organizations need end-to-end visibility to ensure products are authentic, safely handled, and actually reach intended beneficiaries. By assigning unique identifiers to products and capturing their movements in a digital system, stakeholders can quickly pinpoint where an item is at any given time or isolate issues (like a bad batch of medication) and initiate targeted recalls[1]. In low-resource settings, improving traceability boosts trust and reduces fraud, theft, and counterfeiting – common problems that undermine humanitarian efforts[2][3]. Overall, robust traceability systems enhance transparency and accountability, which are core values in Public Health supply chains.
