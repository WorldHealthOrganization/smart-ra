A recurring finding from country experience and global dialogue is that implementation fails not for lack of technology but for lack of someone accountable for the architecture as a whole. The Wilton Park dialogue on digital public infrastructure for health \cite{wilton-park-2026-dpi-health} identified the national digital architect — an individual or small team embedded in government, with the authority to define architectural standards, evaluate conformance, and enforce coordination across programmes and ministries — as one of the most urgent and actionable priorities for countries pursuing DPI-H. This role is not an IT manager, a project coordinator, or a donor-funded consultant. It is the accountable designer of the health system’s [digital infrastructure](../folio-assistant-core/glossary/instance/dpi-h-ra/#dpi-h-ra--dpi-h-ra--digital-infrastructure) — the person who holds the architectural blueprint, ensures every investment conforms to it, and has the authority to say no, or to escalate, when a proposed investment would undermine it.
